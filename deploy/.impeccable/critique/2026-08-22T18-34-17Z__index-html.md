## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Grounded in the product in exactly three places — the point-cloud canvas, the scan-beam/wipe-reveal loading sequence, and the real switchable 3D viewer. All three show genuine craft (independent JS-level prefers-reduced-motion checks, a real iframe load event driving state, a code comment reasoning about linear-vs-eased timing specifically so the motion "reads as a scan"). Outside those three moments, the shell — pill nav, glass cards over gradient blobs, fully-rounded hover-blue buttons, tiny uppercase kickers against huge tight-tracked headlines, a logo-marquee — is the current default B2B SaaS vocabulary, swappable into an unrelated product with just a copy change.

**Deterministic scan**: 2 findings, both in index.css, none in HTML/JS.
- Line 747 (warning, layout-transition): .use-case transitions padding, confirmed genuine — the hover state at the same location actually changes padding-inline, so this is real layout-thrash risk, not a dead declaration.
- Line 633 (advisory, codex-grid-background): .workflow-grid's two-axis hairline gradient matches the pattern signature exactly.

Both findings were reviewed earlier this session during /impeccable audit: the padding-transition was deliberately left as-is (single hover-only row, negligible real-world cost), and the grid background was deliberately kept (earns its place via the blueprint/measurement metaphor tied to "how we scan your space"). Neither sub-agent knew about that prior review.

**Visual overlays**: Not obtained. Assessment B correctly reported no browser-automation tool was exposed in its context and did not fabricate one.

## Overall Impression

The three product-specific moments (point-cloud, scan-beam, real 3D viewer) are executed with a level of care that the rest of the page's competent-but-generic shell doesn't quite match. The single biggest opportunity isn't a craft gap — the very first interactive-looking element on the page does nothing, and the page's actual commercial purpose (the contact form) gets the least reassurance of any section.

## What's Working

1. The point-cloud + scan-beam + wipe-reveal sequence is real, product-specific engineering, not template decoration.
2. Honest system status: the demo viewer's loading state clears on a real load event, status text lives in an aria-live region, EmailJS lazy-loads via IntersectionObserver.
3. Unusually thorough input-method awareness: separate pointer/touch copy, scroll-nav relocates to the mobile thumb zone, @media (pointer: coarse) targets touch capability independent of viewport width.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The hero's play button does nothing** — verified: no click handler exists anywhere in index.js for .teaser-play, .hero-visual, .teaser-glow, or .teaser-badge.
- Why it matters: first interactive-looking thing on the page, next to copy promising "see how we scan reality." Teaches visitors the site doesn't work before they reach the demo that does.
- Fix: point it at #demo, or remove the play-button/video framing until real footage exists.
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P1] No reassurance at the contact form**
- Why it matters: peak-end rule — this is the page's ending and its entire commercial purpose, with no response-time or privacy assurance near submit.
- Fix: one line near the button ("We typically reply within one business day. No spam.") in both locales.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P1] Form validation is native-only**
- Why it matters: browser default validation bubbles are inconsistently styled and don't match the site's visual language, at the highest-stakes form on the page.
- Fix: inline field-level validation styled to match.
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P2] Scroll-nav widget has no position indicator**
- Why it matters: the one navigation aid built for orientation fails the visibility-of-status heuristic it exists to serve.
- Fix: a subtle dot/fraction indicator alongside the arrows.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P2] Header crowds 6-7 targets into the first decision point**
- Why it matters: logo, 3 nav links, language toggle, a plain-text contact link, and the CTA button all compete at once.

## Questions to Consider

1. The first thing a visitor tries on this page does nothing — what does that silently teach them about the parts that do work?
2. The point-cloud and scan-beam are the most product-specific ideas here but both are quiet, low-opacity touches — does the design need to lean harder into "you're looking at scan data"?
